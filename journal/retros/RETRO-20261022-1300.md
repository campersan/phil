## Per row: estimate, fill, or variance?

- **US Core CPI 2.4 (e79065a1f9f7): estimate, and the 10:15Z rule
  covers it.** Cleveland core YoY nowcast was 2.39 and the ladder mean
  was about 2.45. I moved my centre halfway toward the nowcast (2.42) and
  piled mass on the 2.4 bucket (0.46 vs 0.365). The print missed 2.4.
  This is the third US CPI month (Aug, Sep headline, Sep core) where
  leaning on the Cleveland nowcast against the ladder lost. The
  10:15Z rule said "headline". This row shows it applies to core ladders
  too, so I am making that explicit in the playbook. No new method.
- **China CPI 0.7-0.8 (32b8df4ad98d, then d6008af07429): estimate, same
  shape.** Both rows held the 0.7-0.8 bucket above the mid. At the
  10-03 record time TE's calendar said 0.8. By 10-13 the TE consensus
  had moved to 1.0 and I cut to 0.21, but I still kept that bucket 0.065
  over the book "for consensus-error width (unsourced)". The print landed
  outside 0.7-0.8. The superseding row's note itself flagged the extra
  width as unsourced. Lesson: when the consensus moves toward the ladder
  centre, unsourced tail width does not justify sitting above the mid on
  an off-centre bucket. n=1 month for China. Logged as an observation
  under the ladder-centring family, with no separate rule.
- **Eggs $2.20-2.30 (3924d0dc7755): estimate right.** The USDA wholesale
  collapse with a ~1 month retail lag gave a centre of about 2.21. The
  print landed in-bucket. The FRED + USDA lag method is 1-for-1 and
  stays no-edge until n grows.
- **PPI 5.7 (b2dab51a35e8, WSV): estimate right on this bucket.** I held

  Counterfactual (`counterfactual.py ledger --rows`): No side, edge
